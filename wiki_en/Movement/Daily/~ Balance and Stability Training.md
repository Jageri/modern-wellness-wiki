# Balance and Stability Training

**Mortality Impact**: Indirect — falls are the 2nd leading cause of unintentional injury death; grip strength decline per 5kg → +16% ACM. Balance training reduces falls by ~16% (RR 0.84), with significant survival impact in older adults.
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (grip strength-mortality: large prospective cohorts; fall prevention: 54 RCTs)
**Actionability**: High (no equipment needed, home-executable)

## Bottom Line
Balance and stability training does not directly reduce mortality, but it is one of the most effective non-pharmacological interventions for preventing falls in older adults. Grip strength—a simple proxy for overall muscle strength—shows that each 5kg decline increases all-cause mortality by 16%. Balance and stability training should form the foundation of any exercise program.

## Evidence
- WHO data: Falls are the 2nd leading cause of unintentional injury death worldwide (~684,000 deaths annually); adults ≥65 years at highest risk
- **Sherrington et al., Br J Sports Med 2011 / updated Meta-analysis** (54 RCTs): exercise reduces fall rate by ~16% (pooled rate ratio 0.84, 0.77-0.91); best results from balance training + resistance training + Tai Chi [PMID: 21632004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21632004/)
- **Leong et al., Lancet 2015** (PURE study, 140,000 participants, 17 countries): each 5kg reduction in grip strength → +16% all-cause mortality (HR 1.16, 1.13-1.20) and +17% CVD mortality (HR 1.17, 1.11-1.24), outperforming SBP and BMI as mortality predictors [PMID: 25982160](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25982160/)
- **López-Bueno et al., Ageing Res Rev 2022** (48 studies, 3,135,473 participants): grip strength showed a near-linear inverse association with all-cause mortality in the 26-50kg range [PMID: 36332759]
- **Cochrane review (2012 update)**: multifactorial interventions (including balance training) reduce fall rate by ~24% in community-dwelling older adults
- Peter Attia's *Outlive* Chapter 13 positions stability training as the "foundation layer" of exercise: single-leg closed-eye standing, dead hangs (≥2 min), toe yoga, core stability

> **Note**: Balance training's direct survival benefit has not been independently evaluated; its benefit comes from fall prevention. In younger/non-fall-risk populations, direct mortality benefit is unclear. Grip strength-mortality associations may involve residual confounding (declining grip strength may reflect overall health deterioration rather than being causal).

## Do
1. **Balance training 2-3x/week**: start with single-leg stand (30s→60s→eyes closed), progressively increase difficulty. Tai Chi or yoga are excellent options
2. **Maintain grip strength**: dead hang target ≥2 minutes; carry 50% body weight in each hand for ≥1 minute (farmer's walk). Regular grip training
3. **Core stability**: dead bug, bird dog, plank — 30-60 seconds each, preventing exercise injuries and fall-related loss of control

## Don't
1. Don't wait until old age to train balance — balance and grip strength decline 1-2%/year starting in midlife (40-50). Earlier is better
2. Don't forget balance training in your exercise routine — strength and cardio cannot replace stability; they are complementary

## When to See a Doctor
- Unexplained falls (not from tripping/slipping) indicate significantly declined balance — seek evaluation promptly
- Significantly reduced grip strength (men <26kg, women <18kg; relaxed Asian cutoff) suggests sarcopenia risk
