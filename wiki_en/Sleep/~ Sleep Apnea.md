# Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)

**Mortality Impact**: No single percentage is established as an individual causal effect. In a cohort meta-analysis, severe OSA was associated with higher all-cause mortality (HR 2.13, 95% CI 1.68–2.68), whereas the estimate for moderate OSA was not statistically significant (HR 1.28, 0.96–1.69)
**Evidence Level**: Severity–mortality association ⭐⭐⭐⭐; causal evidence that PAP/CPAP reduces death or cardiovascular hard outcomes ⭐⭐ (not confirmed overall by RCTs)
**Actionability**: High (diagnosable with objective sleep testing and treatable with individualized care)

## Bottom Line
Loud irregular snoring, witnessed apneas or gasping, and excessive daytime sleepiness warrant attention, but symptoms and questionnaires cannot establish the diagnosis. Severe OSA is associated with higher all-cause and cardiovascular mortality in observational cohorts; this does not mean that OSA has been proven to double an individual's mortality. PAP/CPAP reduces respiratory events and improves symptoms such as sleepiness, but randomized trials have not established an overall reduction in death or major cardiovascular or cerebrovascular events.

## Evidence
- **Severity and mortality (observational)**: Fu et al. pooled 27 cohort studies with 3,162,083 participants. Compared with controls, all-cause mortality HRs were 1.19 (95% CI 0.86–1.65) for mild, 1.28 (0.96–1.69) for moderate, and 2.13 (1.68–2.68) for severe OSA; only the severe estimate was clearly above 1. Cardiovascular mortality HRs were 1.24 (0.53–2.55), 2.05 (0.57–5.47), and 2.73 (1.94–3.85), respectively. These cohort associations remain vulnerable to confounding by obesity, comorbidity, treatment selection, and adherence.[PMID: 27502205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27502205/)
- **PAP and mortality (mixed study designs)**: A 2025 systematic review included 10 RCTs and 20 confounder-adjusted nonrandomized studies, totaling 1,175,615 participants. In the combined analysis, PAP was associated with lower all-cause mortality (HR 0.63, 0.56–0.72) and cardiovascular mortality (HR 0.45, 0.29–0.72). Because the pooled estimate combines randomized and nonrandomized evidence, it cannot be read directly as a causal treatment effect. The review was funded by ResMed, and several authors reported PAP-industry relationships.[PMID: 40118084](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40118084/)
- **Contrary hard-outcome evidence**: A 2024 updated meta-analysis (10 RCTs and 3 observational studies; 13,832 participants) did not find significant reductions in all-cause mortality (RR 0.92, 0.72–1.16), cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.63, 0.33–1.19), or MACE (RR 0.73, 0.52–1.03).[PMID: 39083193](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39083193/)
- **SAVE trial**: Among 2,717 adults aged 45–75 with moderate-to-severe OSA and established coronary or cerebrovascular disease—mostly men with minimal sleepiness—mean CPAP use was 3.3 hours/night over 3.7 years. The primary cardiovascular composite occurred in 17.0% versus 15.4% (HR 1.10, 0.91–1.32), showing no benefit; CPAP did improve sleepiness, snoring, quality of life, and mood.[PMID: 27571048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27571048/)
- **Adherence signal, not randomized proof by adherence**: In an individual-participant-data meta-analysis of three secondary-prevention RCTs (4,186 participants; 82.1% men), the randomized comparison gave a MACCE HR of 1.01 (0.87–1.17). An on-treatment analysis associated use ≥4 hours/day with lower MACCE risk (HR 0.69, 0.52–0.92), but adherence itself was not randomized, leaving healthy-user bias and residual confounding possible.[PMID: 37787793](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37787793/)

## Do
1. **Seek clinical assessment when symptomatic or high risk**: habitual loud snoring, witnessed apneas or gasping, excessive daytime sleepiness, morning headache or dry mouth, obesity, hypertension, and cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease may justify screening. STOP-Bang can screen risk; the Epworth scale measures sleepiness. Neither diagnoses OSA.
2. **Confirm with objective testing**: polysomnography (PSG) is the diagnostic standard. In uncomplicated adults suspected of moderate-to-severe OSA, a clinician may arrange technically adequate home sleep apnea testing (HSAT). If one HSAT is negative, inconclusive, or technically inadequate while suspicion remains, proceed to PSG.[PMID: 28162150](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28162150/)
3. **Choose treatment using symptoms, severity, and comorbidity**: the Chinese adult OSA guideline recommends PAP as first-line therapy for moderate-to-severe OSA; mild OSA requires individualized decisions based on sleepiness, quality of life, hypertension, and other comorbidities. PAP should follow an objective diagnosis and include monitoring of efficacy, device use, leak, and adverse effects.[PMID: 41820035](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41820035/) [PMID: 30736887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30736887/)
4. **Address adherence barriers early**: for mask discomfort, nasal dryness or congestion, leak, or pressure intolerance, contact the sleep clinic rather than stopping on your own. Guidelines support education, behavioral/troubleshooting help, and telemonitoring during initiation.[PMID: 30736887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30736887/)
5. **Use comprehensive weight management if overweight or obese**: combine reduced energy intake, increased physical activity, and behavioral support. Weight loss generally improves OSA severity, but the amount lost cannot replace repeat assessment, and PAP should not be stopped without reevaluation.[PMID: 30215551](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30215551/)
6. **Reduce aggravating factors**: avoid alcohol before bed and use sedative-hypnotic drugs cautiously. Clinician-guided positional therapy is reasonable for position-dependent OSA.[PMID: 41820035](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41820035/)

## Don't
1. Do not diagnose or exclude OSA using snoring, an Epworth score, a smartwatch/ring, or another consumer device alone.
2. Do not turn an observational association with lower risk into a claim that CPAP has been proven to extend life.
3. Do not treat “4 hours/night” as a universally sufficient target. It is a common research adherence threshold; treatment should cover as much sleep as possible and be judged with device data and follow-up.
4. Do not substitute anti-snoring sprays, nasal strips, or exercise alone for medical evaluation and indicated treatment.

## Driving and Urgent Evaluation
- If you have fallen asleep while driving or had a sleepiness-related near miss or crash, stop driving and seek prompt assessment; coffee, open windows, or “pushing through” are not adequate safeguards.
- Seek prompt care when witnessed apneas/gasping recur or daytime sleepiness is impairing work or safety.
- People in safety-critical occupations or with significant cardiorespiratory disease, neuromuscular weakness, awake hypoventilation, chronic opioid use, prior stroke, or severe insomnia need a clinician-selected testing pathway, usually favoring PSG.
