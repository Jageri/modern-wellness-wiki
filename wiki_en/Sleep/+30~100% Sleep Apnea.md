# Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA)

**Mortality Impact**: Severe OSA +100~200% all-cause mortality; moderate-severe +20~100%
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (multiple prospective cohorts + meta-analyses; CPAP RCT evidence for cardiovascular outcomes)
**Actionability**: High (diagnosable and treatable)

## Bottom Line
Snoring ≠ good sleep. OSA is an independent risk factor for hypertension, atrial fibrillation, and stroke. Severe untreated OSA doubles mortality risk. Observational data suggests CPAP therapy is associated with 34–37% lower all-cause and 55–63% lower cardiovascular mortality (primarily from observational studies; the largest RCT did not confirm cardiovascular benefit).

## Evidence
- Global prevalence: ~936 million adults aged 30-69 (Benjafield et al., Lancet Respir Med 2019), with China having the highest number of cases (11.8% adult prevalence). The diagnosis rate is extremely low (0.9% among high-risk civil servants in China) [1] [PMID: 31300334](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31300334/)
- **Fu et al. meta-analysis (2017)**: 27 cohort studies, 3.16 million participants — severe OSA associated with **+113%** all-cause mortality risk (HR=2.13, 95%CI 1.68-2.68) and **+173%** cardiovascular mortality risk (HR=2.73, 1.94-3.85); mild OSA showed no significant increase [PMID: 27502205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27502205/)
- Same meta-analysis: CPAP-treated patients vs untreated — all-cause mortality reduced by **34%** (HR=0.66, 0.59-0.73), cardiovascular mortality reduced by **63%** (HR=0.37, 0.16-0.54) [PMID: 27502205](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27502205/)
- **Lancet Respir Med 2025** systematic review (Benjafield et al., 30 studies, 1.18M participants): PAP therapy associated with **37%** lower all-cause mortality (HR=0.63, 0.56-0.72) and **55%** lower cardiovascular mortality (HR=0.45, 0.29-0.72); benefit increased with adherence [PMID: 40118084](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40118084/)
  - ⚠️ Study funded by ResMed (CPAP manufacturer)
- However, the largest CPAP RCT—the SAVE trial (McEvoy et al., NEJM 2016, n=2,717)—did not find a significant reduction in the cardiovascular composite endpoint with CPAP (HR=1.10, 0.91-1.32). However, mean adherence was only 3.3 h/night, which may be insufficient to test CPAP's protective effect [PMID: 27571048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27571048/)
- Benefits disappear after CPAP discontinuation — greater adherence is associated with greater benefit [3]
- Key mechanisms: intermittent hypoxia → oxidative stress → sympathetic activation → blood pressure elevation, inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, arrhythmia (especially atrial fibrillation)
- Causal relationship between OSA and hypertension well-established; up to ~80% of resistant hypertension patients have comorbid OSA [Hypertension Research 2024 review]

> **Note**: Mild OSA (AHI 5-15) shows no statistically significant increase in mortality, but often progresses to moderate-severe. CPAP mortality data comes mainly from observational studies as sham-controlled RCTs are ethically problematic. Study populations predominantly middle-aged males; female data is limited.

## Do
1. **Self-screen with triad**: ① Loud irregular snoring (with witnessed apneas/gasping) ② Excessive daytime sleepiness (Epworth Sleepiness Scale ≥10) ③ Morning headache, dry mouth, or hypertension — any 2 of 3 warrants sleep study
2. **CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure)** is first-line gold standard; effectively eliminates respiratory events in moderate-severe OSA during proper use (titrated AHI returns to normal range) [1]
3. **Weight loss**: 10% weight reduction lowers AHI by ~26% (95%CI 18-34%) [Peppard et al., JAMA 2000, PMID: 11122588]; ≥10% weight loss in clinical trials reduces AHI by ~50% [MIMOSA trial, DOI: 10.5664/jcsm.9834]
4. **Positional therapy**: lateral (side) sleeping significantly reduces severity (may normalize AHI in some)
5. **Avoid alcohol and sedatives before bed**: alcohol reduces pharyngeal muscle tone, worsening apnea
6. **Treat comorbidities**: CPAP reduces daytime systolic BP by ~2.2 mmHg (nighttime systolic ~3.8 mmHg); higher baseline AHI predicts greater BP reduction [Fava et al., Chest 2014, PMID: 24077181]

## Don't
1. Don't assume "snoring means sound sleep" — loud irregular snoring is a warning signal
2. Don't rely on anti-snoring sprays or nasal strips — they mask symptoms without addressing the underlying problem
3. Don't rely solely on exercise to treat OSA — exercise helps but weight loss is the key metric

## When to See a Doctor
- Witnessed breathing pauses or gasping during sleep
- Falling asleep involuntarily while driving or sitting still (Epworth ≥16 indicates severe sleepiness)
- Resistant hypertension (≥3 medications still not controlled)
