# Vitamin D

**Mortality Impact**: No significant effect on all-cause mortality (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.95–1.02), but reduces cancer mortality by 15% (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.74–0.97)
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Large RCT meta-analysis + large RCT)
**Actionability**: High

## Conclusion

High-dose vitamin D supplementation does not reduce all-cause mortality, but may reduce cancer mortality by ~15%. Important for bone health and calcium absorption; those who are deficient should supplement. D3 is superior to D2.

## Evidence

- **All-cause mortality**: BMJ 2019 Meta-analysis (50 RCTs, 74,655 participants) — Vitamin D supplementation showed no significant effect on all-cause mortality (RR 0.98, 95% CI 0.95–1.02). D3 subgroup showed borderline significance (RR 0.95, 0.91–1.00, P interaction = 0.04) [PMID: 31405892]
- **WHI 20-Year Follow-up** (Annals of Internal Medicine 2024, 36,282 postmenopausal women, median follow-up 22.3 years) — Calcium + vitamin D (1000 mg Ca + 400 IU D₃/day) vs placebo: all-cause mortality HR 1.00 (0.97–1.03), no significant effect; but cancer mortality ↓7% (HR 0.93, 0.87–0.99), CVD mortality ↑6% (HR 1.06, 1.01–1.12) — the two opposing effects cancel out [PMID: 38467003]
- **Cancer mortality**: BMJ 2019 Meta-analysis — Vitamin D supplementation reduces cancer mortality risk by 15% (RR 0.85, 95% CI 0.74–0.97), observed only in the D3 group
- **Fracture risk**: VITAL trial (25,871 participants, median follow-up 5.3 years) — 2000 IU D₃/day did not significantly reduce overall fracture risk (HR 0.98), suggesting supplementation is unnecessary for non-deficient individuals [PMID: 35939579]
- **Vitamin D deficiency** is clearly associated with increased osteoporosis and fracture risk; supplementation is beneficial in deficient populations (elderly, institutionalized)
- **Vitamin D status in China**: Severe deficiency (<25 nmol/L) ~20.7%, insufficiency (25–50 nmol/L) ~63.2%, ~84% of Chinese adults have suboptimal vitamin D levels (eClinicalMedicine/Lancet 2021)
- **D3 vs D2**: D3 raises serum 25(OH)D more effectively than D2; only D3 showed a mortality benefit trend in the BMJ meta-analysis

## Do

1. Get 15–20 minutes of gentle sun exposure daily (avoid 10:00–14:00 peak UV in summer)
2. If deficient (25-hydroxyvitamin D <20 ng/mL or <50 nmol/L), supplement with D3 at 800–2000 IU/day
3. Food sources: fatty fish (salmon, sardines), egg yolks, fortified milk, UV-exposed mushrooms
4. Those in northern latitudes with limited winter sun should proactively test serum 25-hydroxyvitamin D

## Don't

1. Don't expect vitamin D to reduce all-cause mortality — current RCT evidence does not support this
2. Don't spend money on vitamin D supplements for "anti-aging" — WHI 20-year data confirms no net survival benefit; cancer mortality reduction is offset by increased CVD mortality
3. Don't over-supplement (>4000 IU/day) — may cause hypercalcemia; high-dose calcium co-supplementation may increase kidney stone risk
4. Don't treat vitamin D as a cure-all — insufficient evidence for cardiovascular, diabetes, or other indications

## Warning Signs — See a Doctor

- Bone pain, recurrent fractures
- 25-hydroxyvitamin D <20 ng/mL (or <50 nmol/L)
- Persistent deficiency despite supplementation (rule out malabsorption: celiac disease, Crohn's, etc.)
- Symptoms of hypercalcemia: nausea, vomiting, excessive thirst/urination, constipation

## Caveats

- **Reverse causation warning**: Observational studies often find "vitamin D deficiency is associated with various diseases," but the causal direction may be reversed — it's not that low vitamin D causes illness, but that sick/frail people go outdoors less, leading to lower sun exposure and lower vitamin D levels. RCTs are the gold standard for causality, and RCTs consistently show no significant effect of vitamin D supplementation on all-cause mortality
- **WHI limitations**: The vitamin D dose in this study was only 400 IU/day, below the currently recommended 800–2000 IU/day; calcium was co-administered, so effects cannot be attributed solely to vitamin D
- **Don't supplement if not deficient**: Healthy adults with adequate serum 25(OH)D (>30 ng/mL or >75 nmol/L) gain no additional benefit from extra supplementation
