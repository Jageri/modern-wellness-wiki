# Melatonin

> Last updated: 2025-06-12 | Entry author: Longevity Wiki AI

**Mortality Impact**: ⚠️ Signal conflict — Short-term RCTs show no mortality increase; long-term observational study (AHA 2025) reports HR 2.09 for all-cause mortality and HR 3.44 for heart failure hospitalization, but observational data cannot prove causation
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐ (large observational cohort + short-term RCT safety data; no long-term RCTs exist)
**Actionability**: High (widely available, OTC)

## Bottom Line

Melatonin is effective for short-term sleep regulation (especially jet lag and chronic insomnia in adults >55). Short-term RCTs show no serious adverse events. However, a large 2025 AHA Scientific Sessions observational study (130K patients, 5-year follow-up) found that long-term users had 89% higher heart failure risk and doubled all-cause mortality. **This finding has not yet been formally peer-reviewed, and the observational design cannot exclude confounders (e.g., more severe insomnia itself is a mortality risk factor).** No RCT evidence exists to support or refute long-term cardiovascular safety. Use only short-term and at low doses; avoid as a chronic sleep aid.

## Evidence

### 🔴 Adverse Evidence (Long-Term Safety Signal)

- **AHA 2025 Scientific Sessions — Nnadi et al.**: Retrospective cohort using TriNetX Global Research Network. 130,828 adults with insomnia (mean age 56, 61% women), propensity-score matched on >40 variables. Long-term melatonin users (≥1 year) vs. controls, 5-year follow-up:
  - Incident heart failure: 4.6% vs. 2.7%, **HR 1.89** (95% CI 1.78–2.00)
  - HF-related hospitalization: 19.0% vs. 6.6%, **HR 3.44** (95% CI 3.32–3.56)
  - All-cause mortality: 7.8% vs. 4.3%, **HR 2.09** (95% CI 1.99–2.18)
  - Sensitivity analysis (≥2 fills ≥90 days apart): HF HR 1.82, consistent
  - 📌 Source: *Circulation* 2025;152(Suppl_3). Abstract 4371606. **Conference abstract — not yet formally peer-reviewed**
  - ⚠️ **Critical limitations**: (1) Observational design, association ≠ causation; (2) Melatonin is OTC in the US — control group likely includes many unprescribed users, creating misclassification bias; (3) Did not adjust for insomnia severity, concomitant medications; (4) Selection bias — patients needing a melatonin prescription may have worse insomnia or more comorbidities

### 🟢 Favorable/Neutral Evidence

- **RCT Safety Meta-Analysis — Menczel Schrire et al. (2022)**: 79 RCTs, 3,861 adults, melatonin ≥10 mg/day. Serious adverse events RR 0.88 (0.52–1.50, ns), withdrawals due to AEs RR 0.93 (0.24–3.56). Common AEs (drowsiness, headache) increased RR 1.40 (1.15–1.69). **Most studies <3 months — cannot assess long-term safety.** 29 studies (37%) reported zero AEs entirely. [DOI: 10.1111/jpi.12782](https://doi.org/10.1111/jpi.12782)

- **Pediatric Safety Systematic Review — Händel et al. (2023)**: 22 RCTs (1,350 children) + 4 observational studies. Melatonin did not increase serious AEs, but non-serious AEs increased (RR 1.56, 1.01–2.43). Long-term use (2–4 years) showed uncertain effects on pubertal development. [DOI: 10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.102083](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.eclinm.2023.102083)

- **Cardiovascular Protective Mechanisms (Preclinical)**: Multiple studies show melatonin receptors (MT1/MT2) reduce heart failure and cardiomyopathy risk post-MI via antioxidant and anti-inflammatory pathways. This contradicts the AHA observational signal, highlighting the need for clinical RCTs. [PMC9251346](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9251346/)

- **Veterans 30-Day Mortality Study (2022)**: Retrospective analysis found melatonin associated with 37.9% decreased 30-day in-hospital mortality and 33.5% decreased 30-day overall mortality. [PMID: 35652450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35652450/)

- **Sleep Efficacy Meta-Analysis**: Melatonin moderately reduces sleep onset latency, increases total sleep time, and improves overall sleep quality. [PMC3656905](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3656905/)

### ⚖️ Evidence Summary

| Dimension | Direction | Quality |

## Do

1. **Use only short-term**: Jet lag (0.5–5 mg, 1–3 days before travel + 2–5 days after arrival); chronic insomnia in adults >55 can trial 2–4 weeks
2. **Start at low doses**: 0.3–1 mg (physiological dose), 30 min before bed; commercial 3–10 mg products far exceed physiological needs
3. **Prioritize non-pharmacological interventions**: Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia (CBT-I) is first-line, more effective than melatonin with no dependency risk
4. **Improve sleep hygiene**: Fixed bedtime/wake time, bedroom for sleep only, screen-free 1 hour before bed, bedroom temperature 18–20°C
5. If melatonin is necessary, **reassess every 3 months** — avoid indefinite use

## Don't

1. **Don't use melatonin daily for >3 months** — safety data lacking; AHA observational data raises concern
2. **Don't self-increase beyond 10 mg** — higher doses increase drowsiness/headache with no added benefit
3. **Don't combine with alcohol, benzodiazepines, or other sedatives** — may worsen respiratory depression
4. **Don't treat melatonin as a "natural and harmless" supplement** — it is a hormone affecting immune, metabolic, and reproductive systems
5. **Don't give children long-term melatonin without medical supervision** — effects on pubertal development are uncertain

## When to See a Doctor

- Chest tightness, shortness of breath, or leg edema while using melatonin (heart failure warning)
- Insomnia persisting >4 weeks despite melatonin — screen for sleep apnea, anxiety/depression, hyperthyroidism
- Excessive daytime sleepiness affecting work or driving
- Using melatonin for >6 months — discuss continuation with your physician

## Caveats

- **Confounder explanation**: Higher mortality and HF rates in the AHA study's melatonin group may reflect: (1) patients needing a prescription had worse insomnia, and severe insomnia is itself a cardiovascular risk factor; (2) sleep disorders commonly co-occur with depression/anxiety; (3) OTC use in the control group went unrecorded
- **Dose uncertainty**: Commercial melatonin products show large discrepancies from labeled content (studies report deviations of +478% to −83%), making quality control an unresolved issue
- **Special populations**: Not recommended in pregnancy/lactation; use caution in autoimmune conditions (melatonin has immunomodulatory effects); slower metabolism in hepatic impairment
- **Core problem in the field**: No well-designed long-term RCT has evaluated melatonin's effect on cardiovascular events and mortality — all conclusions about "safe" or "harmful" are speculative
