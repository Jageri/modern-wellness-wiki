# Rapamycin

**Mortality Impact**: No evidence shows that rapamycin extends human lifespan or reduces all-cause mortality; it extends lifespan in mice, with effects varying by sex, genotype, dose, and schedule
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐ (Limited and inconsistent human surrogate-endpoint evidence; no human lifespan or all-cause mortality evidence)
**Actionability**: Very Low (Prescription immunosuppressant; use for healthy-person “anti-aging” is not approved)

## Conclusion

Rapamycin (sirolimus) is an important tool in animal longevity research, but this **does not establish human life extension**. Human evidence is no longer confined to transplant recipients: small randomized trials have enrolled healthy or normally aging adults. Those trials have mainly assessed short-term safety, immune measures, body composition, or self-reported scales. They have neither tested nor demonstrated longer lifespan or lower all-cause mortality, and they have not established an anti-aging dose or intermittent schedule.

## Evidence by outcome level

### 1. Animal lifespan: reproducible signal, not directly transferable to humans

- In a 2009 experiment conducted at three sites, genetically heterogeneous mice began dietary rapamycin at 600 days of age. At the age when 90% had died, lifespan was 14% longer in females and 9% longer in males. The authors noted that the effect could reflect delayed cancer death, slowed aging, or both. [Harrison 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19587680/)
- A meta-analysis of 29 mouse survival experiments found substantial heterogeneity: hazard ratios ranged from 0.22 to 0.92. Pooled HRs were 0.41 (95% CI 0.35–0.48) in females and 0.63 (0.55–0.71) in males; survivorship increased by 14.4% (12.5%–16.3%) in hybrid mice and 8.8% (6.2%–11.6%) in pure inbred strains. Thus, “9%–14% life extension” is not a universal mouse effect. [Swindell 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27519886/)

### 2. Rapamycin in humans: safety and surrogate endpoints, not longevity evidence

- A placebo-controlled pilot enrolled 25 generally healthy adults aged 70–95 and studied rapamycin 1 mg/day for at least eight weeks. Cognition, physical performance, self-rated health, glucose tolerance, and most immune measures did not improve. Several erythrocyte measures decreased, and rash, stomatitis, and gastrointestinal symptoms were reported in the rapamycin group. The small, short study addresses feasibility, not long-term benefit or lifespan. [Kraig 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29408453/)
- A 2024 systematic review included 19 adult studies. Some immune, cardiovascular, or integumentary physiological measures improved, while no significant overall effects were found for endocrine, muscular, or neurological systems. No serious adverse events attributed to treatment were reported in healthy participants, but infections and total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, and triglycerides increased in people with age-related diseases. Populations, diseases, drugs (rapamycin and rapalogs), doses, and endpoints were heterogeneous, and the review did not establish longer lifespan or lower all-cause mortality. [Lee 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38310895/)
- The 2025 PEARL double-blind randomized trial compared placebo with compounded rapamycin 5 mg/week or 10 mg/week for 48 weeks. The primary endpoint, visceral adiposity, did not change (p=0.942); adverse and serious adverse events were broadly similar. Signals in female body composition and self-reported pain were secondary findings, while most other endpoints were not significant. All authors were employees and shareholders of AgelessRx, which provides the relevant service, so independent replication is needed. The trial had no lifespan or all-cause mortality endpoint. [Moel 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40188830/)

### 3. mTOR-inhibitor class evidence: immune biomarkers are not clinical benefit or longevity

- Everolimus, a rapamycin analogue, improved influenza-vaccine antibody response by about 20% in a randomized trial of older volunteers. This is an immune surrogate, not a lifespan or mortality outcome. [Mannick 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25540326/)
- A subsequent phase 3 randomized trial of RTB101, another mTOR inhibitor, enrolled 1,024 adults aged 65 or older. Clinically symptomatic respiratory illness occurred in 26% (134/511) versus 25% (125/510), OR 1.07 (90% CI 0.80–1.42; p=0.65): the primary clinical endpoint was not met despite increased antiviral-gene expression. The study was funded by the manufacturer resTORbio and the US National Institute on Aging; several authors were employees and shareholders. [Mannick 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33977284/)

## Dose, intermittent use, and approved indications

- Healthy-aging studies have used 1 mg/day for at least eight weeks and 5 mg/week or 10 mg/week for 48 weeks. These are **research regimens, not approved anti-aging doses**, and they are not interchangeable.
- Animal data suggesting that intermittent schedules may reduce some toxicities while retaining lifespan effects do not establish a safe or effective human schedule. The optimal human dose, interval, duration, and target blood concentration for anti-aging use are unknown.
- The current US label includes prophylaxis of rejection after kidney transplantation and treatment of lymphangioleiomyomatosis. Anti-aging is not an approved indication. The label recommends therapeutic drug monitoring; self-directed anti-aging use does not bypass that risk-management need. [DailyMed prescribing information, revised May 2026](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ad39eb60-1fa0-4722-8ba1-523194921c2a)

## Adverse effects, interactions, and self-medication risk

- The boxed warning states that immunosuppression can increase susceptibility to infection and possible lymphoma or other malignancies. Other labeled risks include dyslipidemia, edema, stomatitis/oral ulceration, anemia and thrombocytopenia, renal-function decline and proteinuria, impaired wound healing, noninfectious pneumonitis, and reproductive toxicity. Event rates from approved-dose patient populations cannot simply be assigned to healthy people using lower doses.
- Rapamycin is a CYP3A4 and P-gp substrate. The label says to avoid strong inhibitors (for example clarithromycin, itraconazole, or voriconazole) and strong inducers (for example rifampin or rifabutin), and not to take it with grapefruit juice. Cannabidiol can also raise sirolimus concentrations. This is not a complete interaction list.
- Live vaccines should be avoided during therapy, and vaccine responses can be reduced. People who are pregnant, planning pregnancy, breastfeeding, facing surgery or an unhealed wound, experiencing infection, living with liver or kidney problems, or taking other drugs or supplements should especially not experiment without specialist oversight.

## What to do

1. **Do not buy, split, or copy an online intermittent regimen for “anti-aging.”** Human life extension is unproven, and drug interactions and individual exposure vary substantially.
2. If prescribed sirolimus for an approved indication, follow the specialist plan for trough levels, blood counts, lipids, kidney function, urine protein, infection, and wound monitoring. Do not stop it or switch to intermittent dosing on your own.
3. If considering research participation, favor an ethics-approved, preregistered controlled trial with explicit safety monitoring. Keep biomarker improvement separate from fewer illnesses, hospitalizations, or deaths.
