# Hypertension (Uncontrolled)

**All-cause mortality impact**: There is no defensible universal “+50–100%” estimate; individual risk varies with blood-pressure level, age, organ damage, comorbidity, and treatment status
**Evidence level**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ (causal evidence that blood-pressure treatment reduces cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality is strong; the size of an individual's benefit is not fixed)
**Actionability**: High
**Guideline date**: Current to 2026-09-08; diagnostic and treatment thresholds vary by country, age, and comorbidity, so use the latest local guideline and clinician assessment

## Bottom line
Confirm the pattern with standardized, repeated measurements, then combine sustainable lifestyle measures with a treatment plan based on total cardiovascular risk. A normal reading while taking medication usually means treatment is working; do not stop, reduce, or switch medication without the prescriber. Contact the prescriber if low readings, dizziness, or other adverse effects occur.

## Evidence and boundaries
- A 2016 systematic review and meta-analysis included 123 randomized trials and 613,815 participants. For each 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure achieved in the trials, the RR was 0.80 (95% CI 0.77–0.83) for major cardiovascular events, 0.83 for coronary heart disease, 0.73 for stroke, 0.72 for heart failure, and 0.87 (95% CI 0.84–0.91) for all-cause mortality. This estimates the effect of blood-pressure lowering; it does not mean that untreated hypertension versus normal blood pressure adds one fixed percentage to mortality. [1]
- The 2024 Chinese clinical practice guideline uses office blood pressure ≥140/90 mmHg and average home blood pressure ≥135/85 mmHg as corresponding diagnostic thresholds, and states that hypertension should not be diagnosed from a single visit. Classification and treatment-initiation thresholds differ in some international guidelines. [2][3]
- The Chinese guideline recommends a target <130/80 mmHg for people without clinical comorbidity who are <65 years and also for those aged 65–79. In adults ≥80 years, office systolic pressure of 130–139 mmHg may be considered if tolerated. These are not universal self-treatment targets: frailty, cognitive impairment, orthostatic hypotension, impaired organ perfusion, or limited life expectancy require individualized targets and closer monitoring. [2]
- In the STEP randomized trial of 8,511 Chinese patients aged 60–80, a systolic target of 110 to <130 mmHg versus 130 to <150 mmHg reduced the primary cardiovascular composite (HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.60–0.92), but hypotension was more frequent. The result should not be automatically extrapolated to everyone aged ≥80 or to markedly frail patients. [4]

## What to do
1. Use a validated upper-arm monitor and the correct cuff size. For 30 minutes before measuring, avoid exercise, smoking, alcohol, and caffeine; empty the bladder, sit quietly for 3–5 minutes, and support the arm at heart level.
2. When hypertension is newly diagnosed or uncontrolled, measure on at least three consecutive days each week: one morning session and one evening session, with two readings 1–2 minutes apart per session and average them. If feasible, measure in the morning before medication and breakfast, and in the evening before dinner. Once stable, reduce frequency according to the care plan. [2]
3. Use sustainable lifestyle measures for everyone with hypertension: salt <5 g/day, regular physical activity, weight loss when overweight, limited alcohol, no smoking, and a DASH-style eating pattern. Potassium-enriched salt is not suitable for everyone; ask a clinician first with chronic kidney disease, hyperkalemia, or medicines that affect potassium. [2]
4. Whether to start medication, which class to use, and whether to use one or more drugs depend on blood pressure, total risk, comorbidities, kidney function, electrolytes, pregnancy potential, and tolerability. There is no single prescription for everyone.

## When to seek urgent care
- If blood pressure is >180/120 mmHg, stay calm, repeat it with correct technique, and obtain prompt assessment for new or worsening organ damage. Do not double doses on your own to make the number normal immediately. [2]
- Call emergency services immediately if it accompanies chest pain, marked shortness of breath, new one-sided weakness/numbness or trouble speaking, confusion, seizure, major vision change, or severe hypertension with symptoms during pregnancy. A hypertensive emergency is defined by acute organ damage, not by a number alone. [2]
