# Housework

**Mortality Impact**: No generalizable housework-specific effect estimate for all-cause mortality
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐ (direct evidence is observational and varies by population and sex)
**Actionability**: High

## Conclusion

Housework can interrupt sedentary time and add to daily activity, but current evidence does not establish that doing housework prolongs life or support a universal duration or mortality reduction. Direct studies rely mainly on self-report and are inconsistent: some observed an association only in men, another only in women, and another found no independent association for housework.

## Evidence

- A 1999 US cohort combined meal preparation, shopping, gardening, and related tasks into a "productive activity" index. Its survival association cannot be assigned specifically to housework or used to prove cognitive-planning or purpose mechanisms. [1]
- A Hong Kong cohort excluded participants with baseline heart disease, stroke, cancer, or diabetes and included 2,867 adults aged 65 or older. Self-reported heavy housework was associated with lower all-cause mortality in men (HR 0.71, 95% CI 0.56–0.91), but not in women. [2]
- A Singapore cohort compared people reporting at least 420 minutes/week of light housework at both assessments with those consistently below that level. Lower all-cause mortality was observed in women (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.36–0.96), while the estimate in men was not statistically significant (HR 0.62, 95% CI 0.34–1.14). The cutoff was the sample median, not a trial-tested prescription. [3]
- The Spanish Seniors-ENRICA cohorts also associated more self-reported housework time with lower all-cause mortality. Exposure groups were sex-specific tertiles among participants, not reproducible fixed minute targets. [4]
- Contrary evidence: in the Whitehall II cohort of 7,456 middle-aged civil servants, sports and do-it-yourself activity were associated with lower mortality, but housework was the exception. [5]
- Device-measured light-intensity activity is associated with lower all-cause mortality, but a device cannot determine whether movement came from housework, walking, or another setting. It is therefore not housework-specific evidence. [6]

## How to interpret this evidence

- Being able to continue housework may itself mark better physical capacity, health, nutrition, housing, or socioeconomic resources. Residual confounding and reverse causation remain possible despite statistical adjustment.
- Light housework, heavy housework, generic light-intensity activity, occupational physical activity, and device-measured total activity are distinct exposures and cannot share one effect estimate.
- Frailty indices, physical function, glucose, and waist circumference are functional or metabolic surrogate outcomes; they are not proof of improved all-cause mortality or lifespan.

## Do

1. If it is wanted and safe, use meal preparation, tidying, or light cleaning as one way to break up sedentary time; there is no need to chase study-specific cutoffs.
2. Do not use housework as a replacement for aerobic, strength, and balance activity adapted to personal ability.
3. Reduce the load and seek professional advice when injury, pain, marked frailty, dizziness, or cardiopulmonary symptoms are present; prioritize fall prevention during climbing, lifting, and work on wet floors.
4. Do not frame housework as a "health prescription" that older adults or women should carry alone; division of labor should respect ability, preference, and fairness.

## Limitations

- No randomized trial has shown that increasing housework reduces all-cause mortality.
- Most studies use self-report, with inconsistent definitions and dose categories. Gender roles and socioeconomic conditions influence both allocation of housework and health.
- Older-adult cohorts are particularly vulnerable to reverse causation: people in poorer health or with lower physical capacity may already be unable to do housework.
- Cohorts from the US, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Spain represent different populations; sex-specific findings from one cohort should not become a universal recommendation.
