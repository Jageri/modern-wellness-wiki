# Raw Cocoa Powder

**Longevity/mortality impact**: Unknown; no direct human evidence translates raw cocoa powder intake into a change in all-cause mortality or lifespan

**Evidence level**: ⭐⭐ (limited and indirect; short-term surrogate endpoints do not establish longevity benefit)

**Actionability**: Moderate

## Conclusion

“Raw cocoa powder” is not a standardized intervention proven to extend life in clinical trials. Cocoa powder, dark chocolate, and quantified cocoa-flavanol extracts differ in flavanol content, sugar, fat, calories, and contaminant exposure, so evidence for one cannot be transferred to another. If you enjoy cocoa powder, an unsweetened, non-alkalized product can be used as an ordinary food ingredient; do not pursue untested high doses for presumed cardiovascular or longevity benefit.

## Evidence boundaries

- **No mortality trial of raw cocoa powder**: Existing observational mortality studies generally evaluate chocolate, not raw cocoa powder. Chocolate-consumption associations neither establish a causal cocoa-powder benefit nor justify a numeric filename prefix.
- **Standardized extract is not cocoa powder**: COSMOS randomized 21,442 older US adults to 500 mg/day cocoa flavanols (including 80 mg epicatechin) or placebo for a median 3.6 years. The primary composite cardiovascular endpoint was not significantly reduced (HR 0.90, 95% CI 0.78–1.02; P=0.11), and neither was all-cause mortality (HR 0.89, 0.77–1.03). Cardiovascular death HR 0.73 (0.54–0.98) was one of several secondary endpoints, with confidence intervals not adjusted for multiple comparisons; it is not independently confirmatory and cannot be transferred to cocoa powder.[1]
- **Short-term blood-pressure change is small**: A Cochrane review included 35 trials and 40 comparisons (1,804 participants), lasting 2–18 weeks (mean nine weeks). Flavanol-rich cocoa/chocolate reduced mean systolic and diastolic pressure by 1.76 mmHg each. Certainty was moderate, heterogeneity remained substantial, and excluding trials with industry-employed authors reduced the effect.[2]
- **“Raw” does not guarantee a flavanol dose**: A survey of 59 cocoa powders from the European market found alkalization associated with lower phenolic content, while raw/roasted classification was not a determinant of the measured compositional differences. A “raw” label cannot replace a measured flavanol value. “Non-alkalized” only avoids one process that lowers phenolics; it does not establish a trial-equivalent dose.[3]
- **Cadmium and lead vary by product**: In a convenience sample of US retail products tested by FDA researchers, cadmium ranged from 0.004 to 3.15 mg/kg and lead from below the detection limit to 0.38 mg/kg; levels varied with cocoa-solids percentage and origin. This survey cannot establish the safety of every brand or batch, and “organic,” “raw,” or high-cocoa labels do not demonstrate low contamination.[4]

## Practical use

1. If using it for flavor, choose an unsweetened product whose ingredient list contains only cocoa powder. “Non-alkalized” is a processing clue for retaining more phenolics, not a guarantee of clinical benefit.
2. Treat it as food rather than assuming that a fixed gram amount, dark color, or a “raw” label delivers 200 mg, 500 mg, or any other flavanol dose.
3. Consider calories, added sugar, and tolerance of caffeine/theobromine. Dark chocolate generally adds more sugar and fat than unsweetened cocoa powder and is not an equivalent substitute.
4. For frequent or high intake, prefer products that disclose batch-specific cadmium and lead testing. Rotating origins or brands may spread uncertainty but does not replace testing.
5. Do not replace proven diet, medication, or follow-up for hypertension, cardiovascular disease, or kidney disease with cocoa powder.

## Cautions

- COSMOS enrolled US women aged ≥65 and men aged ≥60 without major cardiovascular disease or recently diagnosed cancer at baseline. It cannot be directly generalized to younger people, children, pregnancy, or patients with established cardiovascular disease. Mars Edge provided infrastructure funding and study products, and some investigators reported related industry ties.
- The Cochrane blood-pressure evidence came mostly from small, short trials using heterogeneous products ranging from chocolate to quantified high-flavanol cocoa. Improvement in a surrogate endpoint does not establish fewer myocardial infarctions, strokes, all-cause deaths, or longer life.
- Cocoa powder, dark chocolate, milk chocolate, and supplements have different food matrices. Increasing flavanols through dark chocolate also increases energy, sugar, and fat.
- Cadmium is a cumulative chronic exposure. Children, pregnant people, and those with impaired kidney function should not increase high-cocoa intake for a speculative health effect. Reduce or stop intake if it causes palpitations, insomnia, or gastrointestinal symptoms.
