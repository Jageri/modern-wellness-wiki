# Protein Intake

**Mortality Impact**: Animal protein +0~+9% ACM (varies by source); plant protein -5~-10% ACM; substituting plant for animal protein reduces ACM by 10~24%
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (multiple large prospective cohorts + meta-analyses, no RCTs)
**Actionability**: High (directly actionable dietary choice)

## Bottom Line
"More protein is better" is a misconception. For the general population, **protein source matters more than total quantity**: higher animal protein intake (especially red meat and eggs) is associated with slightly increased mortality, while plant protein is associated with reduced mortality. Replacing animal protein with plant protein can lower mortality risk by 10~24%. However, older adults (≥65) require adequate intake of 1.2~1.5 g/kg/day due to sarcopenia risk.

## Evidence
- **Chen et al., Eur J Epidemiol 2020**: Rotterdam Study + 11-cohort meta-analysis (350,452 participants, 64,306 deaths) — higher total protein associated with higher all-cause mortality (highest vs lowest RR 1.05, 1.01-1.10), driven by animal protein (CVD mortality RR 1.09, 1.01-1.18); plant protein associated with lower all-cause mortality (RR 0.93, 0.87-0.99) and CVD mortality (RR 0.86, 0.73-1.00) [PMID: 32076944](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32076944/)
- **Huang et al., JAMA Intern Med 2020**: NIH-AARP study (416,104 participants, 16-year follow-up) — plant protein per 10g/1000kcal reduced all-cause mortality by 12~14%; substituting 3% energy from animal to plant protein reduced all-cause mortality by 10% and CVD mortality by 11~12%; substitution for egg protein showed the strongest effect (men -24%, women -21%), followed by red meat (men -13%, women -15%); dairy and poultry substitution showed no significant association [PMID: 32658243](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32658243/)
- **Naghshi et al., BMJ 2020** (dose-response meta-analysis, 32 cohorts, 715,128 participants, 113,039 deaths): higher total protein intake associated with LOWER all-cause mortality (ES 0.94, 0.89-0.99); plant protein highest vs lowest reduced ACM by 8% (HR 0.92, 0.87-0.97) and CVD mortality by 12% (HR 0.88, 0.80-0.96); animal protein showed no significant association with all-cause or CVD mortality [PMID: 32699048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32699048/)
- Older adult protein needs: ESPEN recommends 1.2~1.5 g/kg/day for sarcopenia risk; confirmed sarcopenia requires ~1.54 g/kg/day (IAAO technique, Frontiers Nutr 2025)
- Key mechanisms: animal protein (especially red meat and eggs) contains more saturated fat, cholesterol, and heme iron, potentially promoting inflammation and oxidative stress; plant protein is accompanied by fiber and polyphenols and displaces high-saturated-fat foods

> **Note**: Protein-mortality associations are primarily from observational studies; residual confounding cannot be excluded. **Existing meta-analyses show contradictory results** — Chen 2020 found total protein positively associated with ACM (RR 1.05), while Naghshi 2020 found a negative association (ES 0.94), likely due to differences in inclusion criteria and reference populations. The effect of total protein intake on mortality is inconclusive, but the **plant vs animal protein difference is directionally consistent across all analyses**. "High protein" in most studies is defined as 17~23% of total energy, not extreme high intake. Subgroup analyses suggest different effects by BMI category.

## Do
1. **Replace some animal protein with plant protein**: legumes (lentils, chickpeas, soy), nuts, grains; as little as 3% total energy substitution shows mortality benefit
2. **Older adults (≥65) ensure adequate intake**: 1.2~1.5 g/kg/day (72~90g/day for 60kg individual), distributed evenly across meals to maximize muscle protein synthesis
3. **Prioritize higher-quality protein sources**: fish and poultry over red and processed meat; plant sources (legumes, nuts, whole grains) over any animal source

## Don't
1. Don't blindly pursue "high protein diets" — the relationship between total protein and mortality is inconclusive (different meta-analyses contradict each other); both very low and very high protein patterns may be suboptimal
2. Don't equate protein intake with red meat and eggs — egg protein substitution data shows it performs worse than plant protein in mortality associations

## When to See a Doctor
- Unexplained weight loss (>5% in 6 months) or declining grip strength in older adults warrants protein intake assessment
- Patients diagnosed with sarcopenia should develop a protein supplementation plan with a physician/dietitian
