# Dietary Fat Quality

**Mortality Impact**: Replacing saturated fat with PUFA: CVD events -17% (RCT). Higher olive oil intake: ACM -15% (RR 0.85). MUFA per +5% energy: ACM -3% (RR 0.97).
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (multiple meta-analyses + RCTs; replacement effect depends on substitute)
**Actionability**: High (switch cooking oil / food choices)

## Bottom Line
Not all fats are bad, and not all fats are good. **Fat source matters more than total fat intake**: replacing saturated fat (red meat, butter, lard) with unsaturated fat (olive oil, nuts, seeds, fish) reduces cardiovascular events and all-cause mortality. One extra daily tablespoon of olive oil (~15mL) is associated with ~8-12% lower all-cause mortality (dose-response data). The "harm" of saturated fat depends on what replaces it — replacing with refined carbs provides no benefit, replacing with PUFA provides clear benefit.

## Evidence
- **Ke et al., Food Funct 2024** (dose-response meta-analysis, 30 articles, 2.71M participants): higher olive oil intake reduced all-cause mortality by 15% (RR 0.85, 0.81-0.89), CVD mortality by 23% (RR 0.77, 0.67-0.88), and CVD incidence by 15% (RR 0.85, 0.77-0.93). Each additional 10g/day reduced ACM by ~8% [PMID: 38568797](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38568797/)
- **Lotfi et al., Ageing Res Rev 2021** (dose-response meta-analysis, 17 cohorts, 1.02M participants): dietary MUFA intake was inversely associated with all-cause mortality (highest vs lowest RR 0.94, 0.90-0.98), per +5% energy: -3% (RR 0.97, 0.96-0.98); but NOT significantly associated with CVD mortality (RR 0.95, 0.89-1.01) or cancer mortality (RR 0.99, 0.96-1.03) [PMID: 34560281](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34560281/)
- **Hooper et al., Cochrane 2015/2020 update**: pooled RCTs — reducing saturated fat and replacing with PUFA reduced CVD events by ~17% (RR 0.83, 0.70-0.98). Replacing saturated fat with carbohydrate showed no benefit [PMID: 26068959](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26068959/)
- **Harvard NHS/HPFS cohorts (Wang et al., JAMA Intern Med 2016)**: replacing 5% energy from saturated fat with PUFA reduced all-cause mortality by 27% (HR 0.73, 0.70-0.77); replacing with MUFA reduced by 13% (HR 0.87, 0.82-0.93) [PMID: 27379574](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27379574/)
- Key mechanisms: unsaturated fats improve lipid profiles (lower LDL-C/apoB), rich in antioxidant polyphenols (olive oil), reduce inflammation. Plant-derived unsaturated fats also provide fiber and micronutrients.

> **Note**: Olive oil studies are strongest in European/Mediterranean populations — potential confounding from the broader Mediterranean dietary pattern. Different saturated fat sources (dairy vs processed meat) may have different effects; don't generalize across all SFA. Coconut oil is saturated fat (>90% SFA) and should not be considered a healthy fat. Total dietary fat is constrained by total energy intake; very high-fat diets may have unfavorable effects on weight control.

## Do
1. **Replace most routine cooking oil with olive oil**: ~1-2 tbsp/day (15-30mL), suitable for low-heat cooking and cold dishes. Extra virgin olive oil (EVOO) has the highest polyphenol content
2. **Increase nut and seed intake**: walnuts, almonds, flax seeds, chia seeds — rich in PUFA and MUFA, one small handful (~28g) daily
3. **Replace some red meat with fish**: rich in long-chain ω-3 fatty acids (EPA/DHA), at least twice weekly
4. **Limit solid fat sources**: butter, lard, palm oil (commonly found in processed snacks) should be minimized

## Don't
1. Don't equate "low-fat" with healthy — low-fat processed foods often replace fat with added sugar and refined carbs, potentially worse
2. Don't consider coconut oil a healthy oil — it's >90% saturated fat, one of the most concentrated plant sources of SFA
3. Don't go to extremes — very low-fat diets (<10% energy) lack long-term evidence for necessity and safety in the general population

## When to See a Doctor
- Diagnosed hypercholesterolemia (elevated LDL-C) or coronary heart disease — adjust dietary fat structure under medical guidance
- Patients with gallbladder disease or history of pancreatitis should consult a physician before changing fat intake
