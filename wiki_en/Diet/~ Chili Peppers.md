# Chili Peppers

**Mortality Impact**: No reliable causal number established
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐ (Multiple observational cohorts; inconsistent exposure definitions)
**Actionability**: Moderate

## Conclusion

People who regularly consumed chili peppers or spicy foods had lower all-cause mortality in cohorts from China, the United States, Italy, and Iran. A 2023 meta-analysis pooled four cohorts (564,748 participants) and reported an all-cause mortality HR of 0.87 (95% CI 0.85–0.90) for regular versus none/rare consumption [1]. These were observational data that combined different foods and frequencies. They do not establish that chili prolongs life, and `-13~23%` is not an expected benefit for an individual.

## How to Read the Evidence

- **The exposures differ**: US NHANES asked whether participants had eaten "hot red chili peppers" in the prior month and did not measure portion size; adjusted HR was 0.87 (0.77–0.97) [2]. Moli-sani compared chili pepper >4 times/week with none/rare intake; HR was 0.77 (0.66–0.90) [3]. China Kadoorie Biobank asked about "spicy foods"; 6–7 days/week versus <1 day/week yielded HR 0.86 (0.82–0.90) [4]. Sources included fresh chili, dried chili, chili sauce, and chili oil.
- **There is no transferable dose**: Studies classified exposure by yes/no or weekly days/frequency, not a common gram amount, capsaicin dose, or heat level. The meta-analysis could not define an optimal form, amount, or frequency [1].
- **Crude absolute risks are not treatment effects**: In NHANES, crude mortality proportions were 21.6% and 33.6% among consumers and nonconsumers, but adjustment markedly reduced the association. Crude rates in the Chinese frequency groups were also nonmonotonic. Age, region, smoking, alcohol, diet, and socioeconomic differences prevent these figures from being interpreted as absolute benefit caused by chili.
- **Cause-specific results were not fully consistent**: Moli-sani reported CVD mortality HR 0.66 (0.50–0.86), but no reduction in cancer mortality [3]. No cause-specific association reached statistical significance in NHANES [2]. Pooled disease-specific estimates do not replace the individual outcomes.
- **Regional diet and residual confounding matter**: Chili consumers differed in meat, fruit and vegetable intake, alcohol, smoking, activity, region, and socioeconomic status. Chili sauce and oil are also bundled with oil, salt, refined staples, and cooking patterns. Adjustment cannot remove residual confounding, reverse causation, or error from a single self-report.

## What to Do

1. If you enjoy and tolerate chili, use it as an ordinary ingredient; there is no need to force spicy food for longevity.
2. Judge the whole dish. Fresh chili, chili oil, salty chili sauce, and oil-heavy spicy dishes are not interchangeable exposures.
3. Capsaicin capsules and supplements were not the food exposures in these cohorts. No evidence shows that they reduce mortality; do not self-prescribe them for longevity.
4. Reduce or avoid spicy food if it reproducibly triggers reflux, abdominal pain, diarrhea, or anal burning. In a small crossover trial, people with IBS-D had more abdominal pain and burning after 2 g chili than healthy controls [5].
5. Capsicum allergy is rare but severe reactions can occur. Breathing difficulty, throat tightness, generalized hives, or fainting after exposure requires emergency care [6].

## Limitations and Funding

- All mortality evidence is observational. There are no long-term mortality-outcome randomized trials of chili peppers, spicy dishes, or capsaicin supplements. Proposed TRPV1, metabolic, or anti-inflammatory mechanisms do not demonstrate human life extension.
- Moli-sani enrollment received support from the Pfizer Foundation and Instrumentation Laboratory as well as governmental/charitable sources. The funders reportedly had no role in study design, analysis, or publication decisions, and the authors disclosed no relevant relationships [3]. The US study received no specific funding [2], while the Chinese study declared no organizational support for the submitted work or relevant financial relationships [4]. No direct chili-industry funding was identified for the core mortality studies, but that does not remove methodological bias.
- The 2023 meta-analysis searched through 2020 and included only four cohorts. Such a small evidence base cannot robustly assess publication bias. Low statistical heterogeneity does not make the food definitions equivalent.
