# Melatonin

> Last updated: 2026-09-07 | This entry concerns exogenous melatonin, not endogenous melatonin levels

**Lifespan/all-cause mortality effect**: Unknown. No long-term randomized controlled trial (RCT) shows that melatonin lengthens or shortens lifespan in the general population. Existing mortality findings come from dissimilar observational populations and settings, point in opposite directions, and cannot establish causation.

**Evidence level**: ⭐⭐ (conflicting observational signals for mortality and long-term cardiovascular outcomes; more evidence exists for short-term sleep outcomes and safety, but it is not lifespan evidence)

**Actionability**: Moderate. Melatonin may have a role in selected sleep or circadian problems, but it should not be used as an “anti-aging” supplement; daily long-term use warrants review with a clinician or pharmacist.

## Bottom line

Melatonin is not a proven longevity intervention. Meta-analyses of chronic insomnia in adults disagree: prolonged-release melatonin produced only about a **5–6 minute** improvement in sleep-onset latency in some trials, while an age-stratified review found no significant improvement in sleep-onset latency, total sleep time, or sleep efficiency in adults with non-comorbid insomnia. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine therefore gives a weak recommendation, based on very-low-quality evidence, that clinicians not use melatonin for adult chronic sleep-onset or sleep-maintenance insomnia.

Long-term clinical outcomes are also uncertain. A 2025 non-peer-reviewed conference abstract found more heart failure and deaths among insomnia patients with long-term melatonin recorded in electronic health records. Exposure misclassification, insomnia severity, psychiatric comorbidity, and other residual confounding could not be excluded. In contrast, three large prospective cohorts published in 2024 found no higher cardiovascular disease risk among users. Neither design proves safety or harm.

## Core evidence

### 1. Lifespan and long-term cardiovascular outcomes

- **Adverse signal (conference abstract only)**: Nnadi et al. compared 65,414 adults with insomnia and at least 365 days of recorded melatonin exposure with 65,414 matched controls in TriNetX. At 5 years, all-cause mortality was 7.8% versus 4.3%, HR 2.09 (95% CI 1.99–2.18; absolute difference 3.5 percentage points); incident heart failure was 4.6% versus 2.7%, HR 1.89 (95% CI 1.78–2.00; absolute difference 1.9 percentage points). This is a non-peer-reviewed meeting abstract. Actual ingestion, dose, and over-the-counter use could not be established; insomnia severity and some psychiatric comorbidity data were unavailable. [Circulation abstract DOI](https://doi.org/10.1161/circ.152.suppl_3.4371606) | [AHA statement on study status and limitations](https://newsroom.heart.org/news/long-term-use-of-melatonin-supplements-to-support-sleep-may-have-negative-health-effects)

- **Neutral signal (prospective cohorts)**: Li et al. pooled 159,072 middle-aged and older men and women in three US cohorts with up to 23 years of follow-up. Cardiovascular disease risk did not differ significantly between users and non-users, HR 0.94 (95% CI 0.83–1.06). The study was funded by the NIH, but exposure was self-reported as yes/no and did not answer dose, regularity, or all-cause mortality; it therefore does not prove long-term safety. [PMID: 38710189](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38710189/)

- **Opposite mortality signal in a special setting**: In a retrospective cohort of 9,386 hospitalized US veterans with sepsis, Sutton et al. found that the 593 people given melatonin on hospital day 1 had lower 30-day in-hospital mortality (HR 0.621, 95% CI 0.415–0.931) and overall mortality (HR 0.665, 95% CI 0.493–0.897). Acute sepsis, a predominantly male veteran population, and inpatient exposure cannot be generalized to lifespan benefit in the general population; residual confounding remains possible. [PMID: 35652450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35652450/)

- **Cardiovascular RCTs do not resolve mortality**: A 2026 meta-analysis included 14 RCTs and 1,027 patients with cardiovascular disease. Change from baseline in left ventricular ejection fraction improved by 3.95 percentage points (95% CI 1.70–6.20), but final ejection fraction and troponin did not improve significantly. Trials were short, dose and route varied, and only 4 of 14 were at low risk of bias, so heart-failure events or mortality could not be assessed. The review reported no external funding and no relevant author conflicts. [PMID: 42123177](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42123177/)

### 2. Sleep efficacy: possibly small and dependent on population and formulation

- A 2023 systematic review included 22 studies and 4,875 participants (925 received melatonin; the review also included ramelteon and placebo groups). Compared with placebo, prolonged-release melatonin shortened subjective sleep-onset latency by 6.30 minutes and objective latency by 5.05 minutes, and increased objective sleep efficiency by 1.91 percentage points. Most trials assessed acute efficacy. [PMID: 37434463](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37434463/)
- Another 2022 systematic review included 24 chronic-insomnia RCTs. In adults with non-comorbid insomnia, sleep-onset latency, total sleep time, and sleep efficiency did not improve significantly. The authors declared no conflicts of interest. [PMID: 36179487](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36179487/)
- The 2017 AASM guideline, based on 2 mg trials, weakly suggests that clinicians not use melatonin for adult chronic sleep-onset or sleep-maintenance insomnia; evidence quality was very low. This does not mean every short-term circadian use in every population is ineffective. [PMID: 27998379](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27998379/) | [Full guideline](https://aasm.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/PharmacologicTreatmentofInsomnia.pdf)

### 3. Safety: more short-term information, insufficient long-term information

- A 2022 safety review of high-dose melatonin (≥10 mg/day) included 79 RCTs and 3,861 adults, but 37% of trials did not mention adverse events at all and only 4 low-risk-of-bias trials entered the main meta-analysis. Serious adverse events, RR 0.88 (95% CI 0.52–1.50), and withdrawals due to adverse events, RR 0.93 (0.24–3.56), were not significantly different; general adverse events such as drowsiness, headache, and dizziness increased, RR 1.40 (1.15–1.69). Wide intervals and short follow-up do not establish long-term safety. [PMID: 34923676](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34923676/)
- A 2017 assay of 31 Canadian commercial supplements found labeled-content deviations ranging from −83% to +478%; more than 71% were outside ±10% of the label claim and 26% contained serotonin. This small market sample does not represent every country, current product, or brand, but shows that product quality can alter actual exposure. [PMID: 27855744](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27855744/)

## What to do

1. **Do not self-prescribe melatonin for longevity, cardiovascular prevention, or “antioxidant” effects**: these clinical outcomes have not been established.
2. For chronic insomnia, seek a proper evaluation first. The American College of Physicians recommends cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) as initial treatment for adults. [PMID: 27136449](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27136449/)
3. For jet lag, circadian problems, or a clinician-recommended indication, define the goal, formulation, dose, timing, and review date. The evidence does not support one universal regimen.
4. If use is daily and long-term, benefit is unclear, or the dose keeps escalating, review it with a clinician or pharmacist rather than stopping abruptly because of one observational risk estimate.
5. Ask a professional first if taking anticoagulants, sedatives, or other prescriptions, or in epilepsy, liver/kidney disease, autoimmune disease, pregnancy/breastfeeding, or use in children. [NCCIH safety information](https://www.nccih.nih.gov/health/melatonin-what-you-need-to-know) | [NHS interaction information](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/melatonin/taking-melatonin-with-other-medicines-and-herbal-supplements/)

## Avoid

- Do not translate “melatonin recorded in an EHR” into “melatonin caused death.”
- Do not translate short-term sleep, ejection-fraction, or biomarker changes into longer life.
- Do not let “natural” obscure daytime sedation, driving risk, drug interactions, or label-content variability.
- Do not combine it on your own with alcohol, other sleep medicines, or sedating herbs; if already combined, ask a clinician or pharmacist to review interactions.

## When to seek care

- Persistent insomnia with substantial daytime impairment, or snoring/gasping, witnessed apnea, or severe mood symptoms.
- Severe allergic reaction, fainting, marked confusion, chest pain, or breathing difficulty after use requires urgent help.
- Daytime sleepiness that compromises driving, machinery use, or fall safety.

## Evidence boundary

As of 2026-09-07, no adequately long RCT was found that used all-cause mortality or lifespan as a primary endpoint in the general population. The evidence supports neither a reliable longevity percentage nor a causal long-term harm estimate from a single conference abstract. The filename therefore uses `~`, not a positive or negative percentage.
