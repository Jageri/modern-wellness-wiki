# Bipolar Disorder

**Mortality Impact**: Compared with the general population, people with bipolar disorder had an all-cause mortality RR of 2.02 (95% CI 1.89–2.16), a 102% relative increase [1]
**Evidence Level**: ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (systematic review and meta-analysis; all-cause mortality evidence is observational, GRADE certainty was moderate, and between-study heterogeneity was high)
**Actionability**: High (specialist assessment, maintenance care, crisis planning, and physical-health care are actionable)

## Conclusion

Bipolar disorder is not ordinary mood fluctuation. NICE advises against using questionnaires in primary care to identify adult bipolar disorder. Diagnosis requires a professional longitudinal assessment of manic or hypomanic and depressive episodes, interepisode symptoms, functional change, treatment history, and family history. Maintenance care, suicide-risk management, and cardiovascular and metabolic care are all important [5][6].

## Evidence

- A 2023 systematic review included 57 samples and 678,353 people with bipolar disorder; 39 samples with 450,397 patients contributed to the all-cause mortality synthesis, which found an RR of 2.02 (95% CI 1.89–2.16). Suicide mortality was RR 11.69 (95% CI 9.22–14.81), natural-cause mortality RR 1.90 (95% CI 1.75–2.06), and cardiovascular mortality RR 1.76 (95% CI 1.53–2.01) [1].
- Between-study heterogeneity for all-cause mortality was high (I²=96.8%), with a 95% prediction interval of 1.37–2.98. Egger's test suggested small-study effects/publication bias (P=0.036). The authors rated the all-cause result as moderate-certainty evidence using GRADE, so RR 2.02 is an across-study average rather than a fixed multiplier for every patient [1].
- A 2022 review included 13 YPLL studies and 128,989 patients and reported a sample-size-weighted average of 12.89 years of potential life lost (95% CI 12.72–13.07) [2]. Because most studies did not report standard errors or confidence intervals, the authors could not use a conventional variance-weighted synthesis or formally quantify heterogeneity or publication bias for YPLL. The CI used an extrapolated pooled standard error and may not accurately represent the true variance [2]. A later corrigendum reordered four plotted estimates in the male life-expectancy forest plot; it did not change the 12.89-year result [3].
- A 2026 Taiwanese nationwide retrospective cohort compared 11,427 people with bipolar II disorder with 45,708 matched controls. The adjusted HR for all-cause mortality was 1.62 (95% CI 1.47–1.78); the within-sibling estimate was 1.31 (95% CI 1.00–1.72). This supports elevated risk while showing that subtype, comparator choice, and residual familial confounding affect the estimate; the overall RR should not be directly assigned to an individual with bipolar II disorder [4].
- WHO describes bipolar disorder as involving manic or hypomanic and depressive episodes and states that effective care commonly combines medicines with psychological and psychosocial interventions [5]. NICE recommends considering specialist assessment when a person presenting with depression has had at least 4 days of overactivity or disinhibited behavior; urgent referral is indicated for suspected mania, severe depression, or danger to self or others [6].

## Do

1. **Record the longitudinal history** of mood, sleep need, activity, spending, social behavior, judgment, duration, and functional impact; with consent, family observations can help [6].
2. **Use phase-specific professional care** because mania, bipolar depression, and maintenance have different goals and medication choices; use shared decision-making and regular review [5][6].
3. **Create a written crisis plan** listing early warning signs, triggers, personal coping steps, contacts, care locations, and emergency routes, and share it with a trusted relative or carer [6].
4. **Monitor medicines and physical health** at least annually, including weight/BMI, diet and activity, pulse and blood pressure, fasting glucose or HbA1c, lipids, and liver function. Long-term lithium also requires renal, thyroid, and calcium monitoring [6].
5. **Protect regular sleep and daily rhythms** and track recurrence signs such as markedly reduced need for sleep with unusually high energy [5][6].

## Don't

1. Do not diagnose yourself or a family member from same-day mood changes or an online questionnaire [6].
2. Do not start, stop, or change psychiatric medicines on your own. Tell the clinician about previous reduced sleep, excitement, overactivity, and impulsivity [5][6].
3. Do not abruptly stop or sharply reduce medication because you feel well. Seek review when considering discontinuation, pregnancy, or pregnancy planning [6].
4. Do not ignore obesity, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, smoking, or alcohol and other substance use while focusing only on mood [1][5][6].

## Medical Red Flags

- Suicidal or self-harm thoughts, plans, or preparations, or risk of harming someone else: do not leave the person alone; contact local emergency, police, or urgent mental-health services immediately [6].
- Almost no sleep with unusually high energy plus dangerous spending, reckless driving, aggression, or severe loss of function requires same-day urgent assessment [5][6].
- Hallucinations, delusions, severe confusion or behavioral disorganization, or inability to maintain basic safety requires emergency care [5][6].

## Caveats

- The core mortality evidence is observational and may be influenced by illness severity, physical comorbidity, health-care access, smoking, substance use, and social conditions. It does not show that the diagnosis alone causes all excess deaths [1].
- RR 2.02 is a relative risk and does not state any individual's absolute probability of death. Absolute risk depends on age, follow-up, baseline health, and care setting. High heterogeneity and a significant Egger test warrant caution with the point estimate [1].
- The 12.89-year figure is a methodologically constrained cross-study weighted average, not a personal countdown. The review could not estimate bipolar I and II separately and had limited regional representation [2][3].
- One author of the core meta-analysis disclosed honoraria or consulting for Angelini, Lundbeck, and Otsuka; the study also received a CAPES scholarship and publication-expense support from the Baszucki Brain Research Fund. Several authors of the 2026 cohort disclosed relationships outside the submitted work with pharmaceutical companies. These disclosures do not directly invalidate the findings but are relevant to evidence appraisal [1][4].
